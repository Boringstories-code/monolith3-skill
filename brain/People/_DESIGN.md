# People

One rich note per human you deal with, enriched over time. Private. Yours.

This is the part of the brain most worth having and most capable of causing harm, so the rules
around it are tighter than anywhere else.

## What it's for

You meet someone twice a year and can't remember their kid's name, what they're building, or
that they hate being cold-called. The information existed — you just had nowhere to put it.

A `People/` note is what you'd want to read in the ninety seconds before a call.

## What goes in a note

- Who they are, what they do, where
- How they like to communicate, and how they don't
- What they're working on, dated
- Context from your interactions: how a conversation went, what they were worried about
- Relationships: who introduced you, who they work with
- Things not to bring up

Use `_TEMPLATE.md`. The `person-enrich` skill writes these automatically as you mention people
in conversation — no permission prompt, no ceremony. That automatic capture is the only reason
the notes stay current.

## Date everything perishable

"Looking for a new role" is true for a few months and misleading forever after. Write it as
"looking for a new role *(as of 2026-03)*". An undated People note quietly turns into
misinformation, and you won't notice, because it's written in your own handwriting.

## The firewall — this is the load-bearing part

Everything in `People/` is **private context only**.

**It may shape tone.** Be warmer. Skip the preamble. Don't bring up the reorg. Lead with the
number because they're impatient with narrative.

**It never becomes content.** No name, fact, quote, date, or relationship from a `People/` note
appears in:

- any email, document, deck, proposal, or page an outside reader sees
- any published site, app, or post
- the `00-OS/wiki/` — the wiki is the shareable knowledge layer, `People/` is not
- anything shared with another person, including the person the note is about

If a fact needs to be usable in outward-facing work, it needs a source outside `People/`. Learn
it again, from somewhere you could cite.

### Why it's absolute

The firewall matters most exactly when no skill is running to enforce it: you're drafting an
email, and a genuinely useful detail is sitting right there in the note. That's the moment.
The rule is absolute so there's no judgement call to get wrong while moving fast.

A soft version of this rule fails the first time it's inconvenient.

## Third-party information

These notes hold other people's information, much of it shared in confidence, none of it given
to you for storage.

- Keep `People/` on machines you control. Don't sync it to a service you wouldn't hand the
  folder to directly.
- Don't write anything you'd be unwilling to have that person read. Sooner or later, assume
  they will.
- Medical, financial, legal, or family details about someone else: only if you genuinely need
  them, and never in an outward-facing artifact.
- If asked to export, share, or publish anything from `People/`, stop and confirm first. Every
  time.

## What doesn't belong here

- Public figures you have no relationship with → wiki `people/` domain.
- Company and org facts → wiki `work/` or `people/`.
- Your own thoughts and feelings → `Life/`.
- Anything you'd want to cite in your work → somewhere citable.
